This table leaves appreciation out on purpose. Home values here have been basically flat over the past year. The long-run trend is up, and you'll hear that number a lot, but a four-year hold isn't a thirty-year one, and I'm not going to build your decision on a number the market isn't currently giving. If the place gains value while you own it, good — that's a bonus. If the deal only works because you're betting on appreciation, it doesn't work.

Straight answers

When I tell families not to buy.

Your student has under three years left

Closing costs on both ends eat a short hold. Under about three years, renting almost always wins.

A vacant summer would actually hurt

Student rentals turn on the school calendar, not on demand. If two empty months would put you under real strain, this isn't the right buy.

Nobody wants to be a landlord

Someone has to deal with the broken water heater in July. If that's not going to be you, and paying a company to do it eats the margin, the numbers have already decided.

The building is mostly investor-owned

Heavy investor ownership can affect financing and resale. I check that before you fall for a specific unit, not after.
